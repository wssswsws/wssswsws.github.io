**Under construction.** This essay is a draft; I am still checking sources and revising the argument.

I have modest expectations of anything ending in “-athon.” A weekend can be useful for trying an idea, meeting people, and discovering why a proposed solution does not yet work. It is a poor deadline for claiming a theorem has been established.

That is why I disagree with the call in the [open letter about the Mathathon](https://proofsandprompts.com/2026/09/10/open-letter-about-the-mathathon/) to suspend the event outright. The letter's concerns about verification and the burden placed on other mathematicians are serious. I think the more useful question is what standards would let people experiment without passing off preliminary work as a finished result.

The full essay will distinguish discovery from proof, machine-checked correctness from human understanding, and the value of learning mathematics from a race to be faster than a model. It will also address responsibility for checking submissions and communicating what remains uncertain.
